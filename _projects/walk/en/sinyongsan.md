Sinyongsan is an area centered around Hangang-daero in Yongsan-gu. It is home to Yongsan Station, Yongridan-gil, and Yongsan Children's Garden.

After [Seochon](./seochon), [Bukchon](./bukchon), and [Jeong-dong / City Hall](./jeongdong), I decided to explore this area because Sejong-daero eventually leads into Hangang-daero.

However, Hangang-daero isn't quite as suitable for dividing the neighborhood as Jahamun-ro in Seochon or Bukchon-ro in Bukchon. So instead, I decided to use the Amorepacific headquarters, which could be considered a sort of landmark(?), as the midpoint for the walking route.

**Walking Route**

1. Yongsan I'Park Mall -> Baekbin Crossing -> Ramen Restaurant -> Yongsan History Museum -> Yongsan Urban Memory Exhibition Center -> Amorepacific
2. Amorepacific -> Yongsan Children's Garden -> Yongridan-gil -> War Memorial of Korea -> Samgakji Station

## Yongsan I'Park Mall

Route: **[Yongsan I'Park Mall]** -> Baekbin Crossing -> Ramen Restaurant -> Yongsan History Museum -> Yongsan Urban Memory Exhibition Center -> Amorepacific

Today, I started by heading to Yongsan I'Park Mall to see the movie *The Odyssey*.

I wanted to see it in IMAX, but there weren't any seats left at all, so I booked a regular theater instead.

If you're going from Sillim to Yongsan Station, where I'Park Mall is located, taking a bus without transferring is probably the easiest option.

You can take bus 152 or 504, get off at Sinyongsan Station, and cross two crosswalks to reach I'Park Mall.

It was an early-morning screening, and I arrived even earlier than expected, so the movie theater was pretty quiet.

That caramel popcorn looks ridiculously delicious.

If I worked at a movie theater, I'd want to work at the food counter. During my break, I'd grab a huge tub of nothing but caramel popcorn and just shovel it into my mouth.

![alt text](../images/용산cgv\_팝콘.jpeg)

They were promoting *Spider-Man*, which I'd watched a few days earlier.

This was the first Spider-Man movie I'd seen since the first two films starring Tobey Maguire, and I actually enjoyed it quite a lot.

There's a scene where the main character goes to a party, and Tame Impala's "Loser" plays in the background.

I got to hear it through the movie theater speakers, which produced a completely different level of sound compared to the earphones I normally use.

At the very end of the movie, there are scenes capturing everyday life in New York. I thought I'd try something similar today and capture the streets I walked through on camera.

![alt text](../images/용산cgv\_스파이더맨.jpeg)

After watching *The Odyssey*, I saw a poster announcing an animated movie coming out next month.

The art style reminded me of *Summer Wars*, so I think I'll go see it once September comes around.

![alt text](../images/용산cgv\_수상한요괴들.jpeg)

I stopped by the Cine Shop and looked around for a while before coming across the Simpsons family, all smiling brightly.

Maggie Simpson's stretched-out mouth looks less like she's sucking on a pacifier and more like a drain stopper keeping the bathtub from emptying.

Homer Simpson's feet look like horse hooves.

![alt text](../images/용산cgv\_심슨.jpeg)

Yongsan I'Park Mall is divided into Living Park (Dopamine Station), Fashion Park, Taste Park, and The Center.

After leaving the Cine Shop, I headed toward Living Park.

Living Park is a great place for people who enjoy collecting things.

From Bandai Namco, Marvel Store, and POP MART on the sixth floor to Dopamine Station on the third, there are all kinds of character goods and merchandise.

I took photos of Gundam and Evangelion.

![alt text](../images/용아몰\_건담.jpeg)

![alt text](../images/용아몰\_에반게리온.jpeg)

There were more characters I didn't recognize than ones I did, so I kept walking around thinking, "Oh, I see," until I ran into Snorlax.

It was nice to see a familiar face, but I couldn't tell whether it was sleeping or smiling.

Maybe it's sleeping with a smile on its face.

![alt text](../images/용아몰\_잠만보.jpeg)

Inside Dopamine Station on the third floor, there's a Yongsan Tourist Information Center toward the back.

They hand out maps and information about Seoul and Yongsan, so if you're planning to walk around Yongsan, it might be worth picking one up.

![alt text](../images/용아몰\_관광센터.jpeg)

After entering Yongsan Station and coming out of Exit 3, you can see a large construction site behind the station.

This area is planned to become the Yongsan International Business District, with several landmark skyscrapers around 100 stories tall. The project is expected to be completed around the mid-2030s.

Yongsan-gu also plans to introduce Yongsan Gateway, an underground pedestrian route connecting Yongsan Station to Yongsan Park, and has set a vision of turning the area into a future global business hub under the brand "Yongsan Seoul Core."

Beyond the construction site, I could see the E-Land building.

![alt text](../images/한창개발중인\_용산국제업무지구.jpeg)


## Up to Amorepacific

Route: Yongsan I'Park Mall -> **[Baekbin Crossing]** -> Ramen Restaurant -> Yongsan History Museum -> Yongsan Urban Memory Exhibition Center -> Amorepacific

After crossing Yongsan Station Plaza and walking past the now-closed Dragon Hill Spa, I came across a vending machine.

It was the first time in my life I'd seen a vending machine giving away free water.

![alt text](../images/용산구\_무료생수자판기.jpeg)

It was hot and muggy, so I was planning to stop by a convenience store, but thanks to the free water vending machine, I didn't need to and was able to hydrate myself right there.

![alt text](../images/용산구\_무료생수자판기2.jpeg)

The alleyways next to Yongsan Station are packed with atmospheric cafés and restaurants, just like Yongridan-gil.

Actually, I think I like this area even more than Yongridan-gil.

This place has an exterior designed with exposed reinforced concrete.

Looking at the door, it reminded me of the Flintstones.

![alt text](../images/용산구골목길\_고인돌가족카페.jpeg)

This street has both clusters of pretty cafés and restaurants with large signs, as well as places tucked away inside ordinary alleyways like the one below.

It looks like a residential neighborhood, but there are surprisingly quite a few businesses hidden among the houses.

![alt text](../images/용산구골목길\_거리.jpeg)

Route: Yongsan I'Park Mall -> Baekbin Crossing -> Ramen Restaurant -> **[Yongsan History Museum]** -> Yongsan Urban Memory Exhibition Center -> Amorepacific

After filling up on ramen and gimbap, I headed to the next destination.

I took some time to learn a little about Yongsan's history at the museum.

![alt text](../images/용산\_용산박물관.jpeg)

If I had to pick a few keywords to describe Yongsan, they would be commerce, the military, and railways.

During the Joseon Dynasty, Yongsan was located outside Seoul's Four Great Gates. Because the area could be reached by following the river currents all the way to Sungnyemun Gate, it was well suited for commerce and transportation.

During the Korean Empire and Japanese colonial period, Japanese residents settled in the area, and much of Yongsan was used for military purposes.

Yongsan Station, Hangang Railway Bridge—the first railway bridge in Korea—and the Gyeongin Railway, Korea's first railway, were all built during this period.

There's something strange about Yongsan Station. Right next to Yongsan Station, there's another station called Sinyongsan Station. But originally, during the Joseon Dynasty, there was only Yongsan.

After Yongsan Station was built, the commercial district and streets centered around Japanese residents came to be called Sinyongsan, meaning "New Yongsan." The name stuck and eventually became the name we use today.

After liberation, the military land once used by Japan was taken over by the U.S. military. Today, the U.S. military base has been relocated to Pyeongtaek, and some of the former base area has been transformed into Yongsan Park and opened to the public.

A quick shot from the museum's rooftop garden.

![alt text](../images/용산\_용산박물관2.jpeg)

It was a nice place to get a quick introduction to Yongsan.

As soon as I entered the first floor, I saw a giant balloon.

![alt text](../images/용산\_아모레퍼시픽1층.jpeg)

The Amorepacific Museum of Art is located on B1, but it was preparing for a solo exhibition by Sol LeWitt opening in September, so I'll save it for my next visit.

I only looked around the first and third floors before heading to Yongsan Children's Garden.

## From Amorepacific Onward

Route: Amorepacific -> **[Yongsan Children's Garden]** -> Yongridan-gil -> War Memorial of Korea -> Samgakji Station

Yongsan Children's Garden was temporarily opened to the public in 2023, for the first time in about 120 years.

If you leave through the back entrance of Amorepacific, the road leading to Yongsan Children's Garden begins almost immediately.

Along the way, I came across dozens of funeral wreaths.

Recently, there was a major controversy after the government proposed building a large-scale rental housing complex on the site of Yongsan Children's Garden. I assume the wreaths were placed to express opposition to the government's proposal.

I heard a little child in the photo tell their mother that the flowers were pretty, without knowing what they had actually been placed there for.

On the far right of the photo, you can see a warning notice from Yongsan-gu.

![alt text](../images/용산어린이정원\_근조화환.jpeg)

Yongsan Children's Garden is quite large. There are exhibition halls, a library, plazas, and even a shuttle that takes visitors around the site.

I especially liked the library among all the facilities here.

I think I'll come back someday when I want to work for an hour or two or just read a book.

Route: Amorepacific -> Yongsan Children's Garden -> Yongridan-gil -> **[War Memorial of Korea]** -> Samgakji Station

After leaving Yongridan-gil, I could see Samgakji Station.

I headed to the nearby War Memorial of Korea.

The statue at the entrance is called *The Statue of Brothers*.

Apparently, during the Korean War, two brothers were sent into battle—one fighting for the South Korean military and the other for North Korea—and eventually encountered each other on the battlefield.

They pointed their guns at each other, but after recognizing one another's faces, they embraced. What happened to them after that is unknown.

The pointy thing on top of the statue isn't part of the sculpture. It's a crow.

![alt text](../images/전쟁기념관\_형제.jpeg)

This is the view of Namsan from the War Memorial of Korea.

The wind happened to be blowing, so all the flags were fluttering.

![alt text](../images/전쟁기념관\_남산.jpeg)

The site of the War Memorial of Korea was also used as a Japanese military base during the colonial period and was later occupied by the U.S. military government after liberation.

It was eventually used as the headquarters of the Republic of Korea Army. After the Army Headquarters was moved to Gyeryong, the site was transformed into the War Memorial of Korea as it is today.

It was almost closing time, so I only took a quick look around.

![alt text](../images/전쟁기념관\_전투기.jpeg)

There's also a library-like cultural space here, which looked like another good place for doing some short work or reading, much like the library at Yongsan Children's Garden.

There was a summary about General Paik Sun-yup.

General Paik Sun-yup is regarded as a national hero for his role in defending South Korea during the Korean War, but controversy over his past collaboration with Japan has continued because he served in the Gando Special Force, a unit involved in suppressing Korean independence fighters during the Japanese colonial period.

On the other hand, there was a man named Kim Won-bong. He was active in the Korean independence movement through the Heroic Corps during Japanese colonial rule and later led the Korean Volunteer Corps, but defected to North Korea after liberation.

A pro-Japanese collaborator but a war hero who defended South Korea. An independence hero but later a collaborator with the North Korean regime.

What a complicated mess.

![alt text](../images/전쟁기념관\_백선엽장군.jpeg)

This is the view of Namsan from up there.
