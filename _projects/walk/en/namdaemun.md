Today, I'm heading to Hoehyeon-dong. It's home to the most famous gate among Seoul's four main city gates.

The area is divided roughly along Toegye-ro into the parts closer to Namsan and those closer to Sungnyemun Gate (Namdaemun).

I'll start at Seoul Station and explore the area closer to Namsan first.

Walking Route

1. Seoul Station → Namdaemun Presbyterian Church → Namsan Park → Shinsegae Supermarket → Piknic
2. Namdaemun Kalguksu/Galchi Alley → Namdaemun Market → Sungnyemun Gate → Bank of Korea Money Museum, Shinsegae Department Store Main Store

I can see the tent promoting a drama that I saw when I went to [Yeouido](./yeouido#saetgang-ecological-park--yeouido-park) for a walk at the beginning of the month. Saetgang Ecological Park is underneath the bridge in the photo.

After getting off the subway, I take the escalator up to the main entrance of Seoul Station.

Right in front of the main entrance, I can see Seoul Square and Seoul Namdaemun Police Station.

![alt text](../images/남대문_서울역.jpeg)

There's a Zara store before you enter the station.

I leave the exhibition.

Seoul Station 7017 is a pedestrian walkway with floor-to-ceiling glass on both sides, making it a nice place to walk while looking around the surrounding area.

Looking to the left, I can see Sungnyemun Gate.

![alt text](../images/남대문_서울로7017.jpeg)

When the World Garden Festival was held at Boramae Park last year, I collected some seeds from these flowers and bought a pot to grow them. Unfortunately, they never bloomed.

## Namsan Park

After walking down the hill from Namdaemun Presbyterian Church, I head toward the spot where the Seoul Station 7017 overpass comes into view. I can see the word "Jung-gu" written there.

After crossing at the crosswalk and taking an elevator, there's a path leading up to Namsan.

![alt text](../images/남대문_중구.jpeg)

I can see the Seoul Station 7017 walkway and the old Seoul Station building behind it.

This is the end of Seoul Station 7017 on this side. On the other side, it ends at Mallijae-ro behind Seoul Station.

![alt text](../images/남대문_남산가는길.jpeg)

There are already tons of ginkgo fruits lying on the ground. The leaves haven't changed color yet, so I wondered if this was normal.

I walked around avoiding them like landmines while putting up with the awful smell.

![alt text](../images/남대문_남산가는길2.jpeg)

After a short walk, I reach the entrance to Namsan Park.

This is both a path leading up Namsan and Baekbeom Square Park.

![alt text](../images/남대문_남산공원.jpeg)

The hill is steeper than I expected.

It was hot, but that also meant I could capture plenty of beautiful sights with my camera.

![alt text](../images/남대문_남산공원2.jpeg)

![alt text](../images/남대문_남산공원3.jpeg)

![alt text](../images/남대문_남산공원4.jpeg)

When I reached the park, I could see N Seoul Tower and a white building.

I think that was Namsan Science Center, or something like that. It seemed to be used as an interactive space for children.

![alt text](../images/남대문_남산공원5.jpeg)

![alt text](../images/남대문_남산공원6.jpeg)

A statue of Baekbeom Kim Gu stands proudly in the park.

Besides Baekbeom Kim Gu, Namsan Park also has statues, memorial stones, and monuments related to Udang Yi Hoe-yeong and Thomas An Jung-geun.

![alt text](../images/남대문_남산공원7.jpeg)

![alt text](../images/남대문_남산공원8.jpeg)

There was also the An Jung-geun Memorial Hall, so I went inside.

![alt text](../images/남대문_안중근의사기념관.jpeg)

As soon as I entered, I saw a statue of An Jung-geun, with a Taegeukgi bearing the words "Korean Independence" behind it.

There were artificial rose of Sharon flowers placed at his feet. I've been thinking this lately, but artificial flowers can look just as beautiful as real ones.

Sometimes I even find myself more drawn to artificial flowers than real ones.

![alt text](../images/남대문_안중근의사기념관2.jpeg)

There were permanent and special exhibitions spread across the first and second floors.

They covered everything from the situation in Korea around the time An Jung-geun was born to his life, the assassination of Itō Hirobumi in Harbin, and his execution.

This is the model of the handgun used to assassinate Itō Hirobumi.

![alt text](../images/남대문_안중근의사기념관3.jpeg)

An Jung-geun was imprisoned at Lushun Prison in China, where he was executed.

It is said that the Japanese authorities secretly buried his remains somewhere around Lushun Prison because they feared that returning them would turn into another rallying point for the independence movement.

But even after 100 years, his remains have never been recovered, and there is only a symbolic grave at Hyochang Park.

Near the An Jung-geun Memorial Hall is the Hanyangdoseong Historic Site Museum.

You can see how stone walls were built to create the fortress during the Joseon Dynasty.

![alt text](../images/남대문_한양도성유적전시관.jpeg)

Apparently, there used to be the Namsan Botanical Garden and a fountain here in the 1960s, and many people came to visit.

The botanical garden was demolished, but the fountain was preserved as a nostalgic landmark.

![alt text](../images/남대문_한양도성유적전시관2.jpeg)

When you leave the Hanyangdoseong Historic Site Museum, there's a staircase going down on the right.

I thought the staircase looked strangely familiar, and then I realized I'd seen it on *Infinite Challenge* in the "Let's Become Friends" segment with Jung Hyung-don and Haha, as well as in the drama *My Lovely Sam Soon*.

## Sungnyemun Gate

After filling my stomach and leaving the alley, I can see Sungnyemun Gate.

It looked like it was undergoing maintenance work.

![alt text](../images/남대문_숭례문.jpeg)

I went through the gate and took a photo of the plaque.

The plaque on Sungnyemun is unusual in that it is mounted vertically.

I looked up why, and according to Korean feng shui, Gwanaksan Mountain was considered a mountain of fire, so the plaque was placed vertically to protect Hanyang from it.

The character *sung* (崇) in Sungnyemun resembles rising flames, while *rye* (禮) is associated with fire in the theory of the Five Elements. The idea was therefore to counter fire with fire.

I used to think things like this were nothing more than superstition and had no real purpose, but lately I've started to find these kinds of beliefs quietly amusing in their own small way.

![alt text](../images/남대문_숭례문2.jpeg)

## Bank of Korea Money Museum, Shinsegae Department Store Main Store

Now I head to the final part of today's walk.

On the way, I saw nuts and snacks laid out all along the sidewalk, starting from the subway entrance.

I looked through them one by one and ended up wanting some chocolate-covered sunflower seeds, so I bought a pack.

![alt text](../images/남대문_길거리.jpeg)

An elderly man was selling them, and he thanked me.

He said he had served in the Vietnam War for two years in the past. I felt like I was the one who should have been thanking him.

![alt text](../images/남대문_길거리2.jpeg)

I stopped by the Bank of Korea Money Museum to look at some money.

These days, I rarely use cash, so it was nice to see all those bills again after a long time. I think the ₩50,000 note has the prettiest color.

The museum also explained some basic concepts about central banks and displayed currencies and commemorative coins from various countries.

![alt text](../images/남대문_화폐박물관.jpeg)

![alt text](../images/남대문_화폐박물관2.jpeg)

When you leave the Money Museum, Shinsegae Department Store's main store is right across the street.

I'll have to come back in winter to see the Christmas decorations. But first, I hope this heat goes away soon.

![alt text](../images/남대문_신세계백화점본점.jpeg)
